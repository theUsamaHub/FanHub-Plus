# Admin demonstration — Dashboard and publishing

Part one of the admin video. Read **Narration** aloud; **On screen** describes what to demonstrate. The sequence follows how related records are prepared, rather than the sidebar's visual order. Continue with file 04.

## 1. Introduction and Dashboard

**On screen:** Sign in as an administrator. Show the dashboard statistics, moderation queues, charts, Platform pulse, and quick actions. Briefly collapse the sidebar and switch theme.

**Narration:**

Welcome to the administration side of FanHub Plus. This panel supports the public website by managing its content, community activity, communication, and system tools. I will demonstrate it in a practical order: first preparing categories and media, then creating related content, and finally managing community and system activity.

The dashboard provides a starting overview of the platform. It brings together statistics, content status, user growth, and items needing attention, including submissions, reviews, and feedback. Quick actions lead directly to common tasks such as uploading media or adding content.

The design keeps the FanHub Plus identity while organising information into compact cards, tables, and charts. The grouped sidebar separates daily publishing tasks from community and system management.

## 2. Categories — establish the fandom structure

**On screen:** Open Categories, show its list, open Create Category, then inspect an existing category's detail and edit pages.

**Narration:**

Categories come first because they organise the fandoms used throughout the website. A category has a name, description, optional custom slug, and an icon. The slug provides its readable address, and the image helps identify it visually.

Once a category exists, other records can be connected to it. These same category relationships also support the favourite-fandom preferences used on the public side. The list, detail, and edit pages let the administrator inspect and maintain this structure.

## 3. Media — prepare reusable assets

**On screen:** Open Media, show upload controls and filters, open an image's edit page, and demonstrate pagination. Show preview and download controls without deleting an asset in use.

**Narration:**

The Media Library stores the images, video, audio, and documents used by the platform. Files can be associated with a category or kept in the general collection. The upload form also supports descriptive alternative text and duration information for relevant media.

Search, type and category filters, and pagination help manage a growing library. The administrator can inspect files, update their details, and download them. Preparing media here allows it to be selected in other forms, keeping content creation organised around reusable assets.

## 4. Tags — add discovery labels

**On screen:** Open Tags, show the list and create/edit forms.

**Narration:**

Tags provide another layer of organisation for content. Categories identify the wider fandom, while tags can describe a more specific topic. I prepare these labels before adding a story so they are ready to select in the content form.

## 5. Content — create, connect, and publish

**On screen:** Open Content and demonstrate its list filters. Open Create Content and show the category, type, editorial fields, media picker, tags, and publishing controls. Inspect an existing record's detail and edit pages.

**Narration:**

The Content module brings these foundations together. The administrator chooses a category and a content type, such as an article, video, audio, or image. The form collects the title, excerpt, body, and relevant media.

The media picker helps connect previously uploaded assets, while tags support discovery. Publishing controls include the record's status, publication time, and featured setting. This separates preparation from publication and lets selected stories receive additional emphasis on the website.

Release dates and labels also provide information used for upcoming content. The detail page lets the administrator inspect the saved record, and the edit page supports updates as information changes.

## 6. Characters — connect personalities to their worlds

**On screen:** Open Characters, then create/edit and detail pages. Show category, image, descriptive fields, and related-content selection.

**Narration:**

Characters are added after content so their relationships can be selected during setup. A character profile connects its information and image to a fandom, and it can also be linked to relevant content.

These relationships help the public website connect a character with the stories around it. The administrator can inspect the profile and maintain its related content instead of treating each page as an isolated record.

## 7. Events — manage real-world experiences

**On screen:** Open Events and its create/edit form. Show location, schedule, publishing, cover, and gallery sections, then an existing event's detail page.

**Narration:**

Events bring together a title, description, category, event type, and optional related content. The location section includes the city, venue, address, map link, and coordinates. The schedule defines when the event takes place.

Cover and gallery images provide its visual presentation. Status, featured placement, and the official ticket link support how the event appears to visitors. Accurate location information also supports nearby discovery on the public side.

## 8. Merchandise — build the connected catalogue

**On screen:** Open Merchandise, then create/edit and detail pages. Select a category and related content to show how character choices depend on those relationships.

**Narration:**

Merchandise follows content and characters because an item can be connected to those records. The form includes its name, category, description, image, edition label, and release information, with an optional character relationship.

These connections give visitors context about the world an item belongs to. The current module supports a discovery catalogue and saved items on the public side. Its focus here is maintaining accurate product information and relationships.

## 9. Recycle Bin — recover removed categories

**On screen:** Open Recycle Bin. Use a disposable demonstration category if showing removal and restoration; otherwise explain the visible controls.

**Narration:**

The Recycle Bin shown here is for removed categories. It provides a recovery step through restoration, alongside a separate permanent-delete action. This gives category management a way to recover from an accidental removal.

With the publishing structure in place, I will now move to the community and communication tools.
